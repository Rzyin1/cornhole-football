Rules & How to Play

Cornhole played like football: move the ball down the field, earn first downs, play defense, use special teams, and score in the end zone.

1. Starting the Game

Before the game, the teams play Rock, Paper, Scissors.
The winner chooses who receives the opening kickoff.
The other team performs the opening kickoff using the normal kickoff rules.

2. Opening Kickoff & Return

The kicking team throws 4 airmails. Each successful airmail moves the receiving team's starting position back 10 yards. Four made airmails pins the receiving team at its own 1-yard line.
The receiving team then begins its return from that spot.
The receiving team throws airmails one at a time. Every made airmail returns the ball 10 yards.
After every make, the receiving team keeps throwing. The first miss ends the return.
The ball is placed at the resulting field position and the receiving team becomes the offense.

There are no touchbacks.

3. First Offensive Possession

The receiving team begins its possession 1st & 10 from the field position determined by the kickoff return.
The offense always throws the first bag. The defense throws second, and the teams alternate throws until both sides have thrown all 4 bags.
A complete round of 8 bags — 4 offensive and 4 defensive — counts as one down.

Yardage

4 YDSOffensive bag in the hole
3 YDSDefensive bag in the hole
1 YDAny bag left on the board
Missed bags: 0 yards.
Net yardage: Offensive yards − defensive yards = yards gained or lost on the down.
Example: Offense makes 2 holes and 1 board bag = 9 yards. Defense makes 1 hole and 1 board bag = 4 yards. The offense gains 5 yards.

Downs & First Downs

The offense has 4 downs to gain 10 yards. If the offense reaches the line to gain, it earns a new 1st & 10.
If the offense does not reach the line to gain, play advances to the next down.
If the offense fails to gain the required yardage by the end of 4th down, it is a turnover on downs. The other team takes possession at the current line of scrimmage and starts 1st & 10.

Defensive Turnovers

Turnover: If the defense throws a bag that knocks an offensive bag completely off the board and the defensive bag goes into the hole, it results in an immediate turnover. The defensive team takes possession at the current line of scrimmage and starts 1st & 10.

Negative Yardage

If the defense scores more yardage than the offense on a down, the difference is negative yardage and the offense moves backward by that amount.
If negative yardage moves the ball into the offense's own end zone, the result is a safety.

4. Touchdowns & Conversions

Touchdown — 6 points: If the offense's net yardage reaches or crosses the opponent's goal line, the play results in a touchdown. Any yardage beyond the goal line is ignored.
Example: If the ball is at the opponent's 3-yard line and the offense gains 7 yards, the result is a touchdown worth 6 points. The extra 4 yards have no effect.

Setting the Board for the Conversion

After the touchdown, the defense places four bags on the board, arranged so there is a clear lane leading to the hole. The offense then chooses whether to attempt the 1-point extra point or the 2-point conversion.

Extra Point — 1 Point

For the extra point, the offense attempts to slide the bag through the open lane and into the hole. If the bag goes into the hole, the offense receives 1 point. If it misses, no extra point is awarded.

Two-Point Conversion — 2 Points

For the 2-point conversion, the offense must throw an airmail into the hole.
The airmail cannot touch the front portion of the board before reaching the hole. If it does, the 2-point attempt is no good.
The bag may hit the back side of the hole and still count as a successful 2-point conversion, as long as it finishes in the hole.
A successful airmail is worth 2 points. A missed or unsuccessful attempt is worth 0 points.

5. Field Goals

Red zone only: A field goal may only be attempted when the offense is at the opponent's 20-yard line or closer.
Declare the attempt: The offensive player must declare that they are attempting a field goal before the round begins.
Clear board: A field goal is similar to an extra-point attempt, except no defensive bags are placed on the board. The board is completely clear.
One throw: The offense gets one field-goal throw. If the bag goes into the hole, the field goal is good and the offense is awarded 3 points.
No good: If the bag misses the board or stays on the board, the field goal is no good and no points are awarded.
Missed field goal: Possession changes immediately. The opposing team starts 1st & 10 at the current line of scrimmage where the field goal was attempted.
Made field goal: After the 3 points are awarded, the scoring team kicks off to the opponent using the normal kickoff and kick-return rules.

6. Kickoffs & Kick Returns

After a score or whenever a kickoff is required, use the same kickoff and return procedure described in the opening kickoff: 4 kicking-team airmails at 10 yards each, followed by receiving-team airmails at 10 return yards each until the first miss.

7. Punts & Punt Returns

Punt: The punting team throws 4 bags. The bags may be thrown as slides, airmails, or any other legal cornhole throw.
10 YDSPunt bag in the hole
5 YDSPunt bag on the board
0 YDSPunt bag off the board
Punt distance: Add the yardage from all four bags to determine the total distance of the punt.
1-yard-line limit: If the punt distance would carry the ball into or through the receiving team's end zone, the punt is stopped at the receiving team's own 1-yard line. There is no touchback.

Punt Return

After the punt is placed, the receiving team returns the punt using the same return system as a kickoff.
The returner throws airmails only, one at a time. Each successful airmail returns the ball 10 yards.
After each successful airmail, the returner continues throwing. The first miss ends the punt return.
After the return ends, the receiving team begins 1st & 10 from the resulting field position.

8. Safety

Safety — 2 points: If the offense loses enough yardage on a down that the ball crosses into its own end zone, a safety occurs. The defensive team is awarded 2 points.
Example: The offense has the ball at its own 1-yard line. If the result of the down is a loss that moves the ball into the offense's own end zone, the defense scores a safety.
After the safety: The team that gave up the safety must kick off to the team that scored the safety. The normal kickoff and kick-return rules are then used.

9. Game Clock

A regulation game consists of 4 quarters of 20 minutes.
Continuous clock: Once the quarter clock is running, it continues to run throughout normal play. The clock does not automatically stop between downs, while scoring a round, or while setting up the next round.
The continuous clock makes clock management part of the game, especially late in each half. Teams may use their timeouts to stop the clock and protect playing time.
End of a quarter — finish the full play: If the clock reaches 0:00 while a round is already being played, that round is finished normally. All 4 offensive bags and all 4 defensive bags are thrown, and the result of the down still counts.
Any result created by the final round must fully play out. If the final round produces a touchdown, the touchdown counts and the offense still gets its extra-point or 2-point conversion attempt. If that score requires a kickoff, the kickoff and kick return are also completed normally. A returner continues making 10-yard airmail returns until the first miss, just like any other return.
The same principle applies to any other consequence of the final round, including a safety or another possession-changing result. The entire sequence continues until the game naturally reaches the point where a new normal offensive down would begin.
No new timed round begins at 0:00. Once the final play and every resulting conversion, kickoff, return, or other required sequence has completely resolved, the quarter ends and the game advances normally.
Q1 to Q2: Possession, field position, down, and distance all carry over. The clock resets to 20:00 and there is no kickoff.
Halftime: After Q2, the first-half possession ends. Both teams reset to 4 timeouts. The team that received the opening kickoff kicks off to begin the second half, so the team that kicked to start the game receives the Q3 kickoff. The clock stays stopped during the kickoff and return and begins running when the return ends.
Q3 to Q4: Possession, field position, down, and distance carry over unchanged. The clock resets to 20:00 and there is no kickoff.

10. Timeouts

Each team receives 4 timeouts per half. Timeouts reset to 4 at halftime.
Clock timeout: When a timeout is called, the game clock stops. When play resumes, the clock remains stopped for the entire following round — all 4 offensive bags and all 4 defensive bags.
After that round is completed and scored, the game returns to the normal continuous-clock format.
A timeout also allows players to walk down and inspect the board.
The defense may call a timeout before the offense releases its bag, including to ice a declared field goal or conversion attempt.
Each team receives 3 timeouts in overtime.

11. Penalties

Foul-Line Violation — 5 yards: A player must remain behind the foul line until the thrown bag has completely finished moving — stopped on the board, gone into the hole, or finished off the board. If the player crosses early, the thrown bag is dead, does not count, and is removed from play. Any of the offending team's bags knocked into the hole by the illegal bag also do not count and are removed. Any opponent bag knocked into the hole counts normally.
Throwing Out of Turn — 5 yards: The illegal bag is dead, does not count, and is removed. Any of the offending team's bags knocked into the hole by that illegal throw do not count and are removed. Any opponent bag knocked into the hole counts normally.
Touching or Moving Live Bags — 10 yards: If a player touches or moves bags before the round is complete, any moved or affected bags belonging to the offending player/team do not count and are removed from play. Any moved or affected opponent bags are counted as bags in the hole. After the penalty is enforced, play resumes normally.
Interference with an Opponent's Throw — 15 yards + automatic 4-bagger: The opponent receives an automatic 4-bagger and the round ends immediately. Bags already thrown by the offending team count normally whether they are in the hole or on the board. Any bags the offending team had not yet thrown count as 0. Calculate the round result, enforce the additional 15-yard penalty, and resume play normally.
Delay of Game — 5 yards: Intentionally delaying or unnecessarily stalling play results in a 5-yard penalty. After enforcement, play resumes normally.
Special-teams throw requirements: When a play requires an airmail, a throw that does not meet that requirement simply does not count. It is not an additional penalty.

12. Overtime

When overtime begins: If all four quarters are complete and the score is tied, the game goes to overtime.
No time limit: Overtime has no game clock. Play continues until the overtime rules produce a winner.
Starting overtime: The teams play Rock, Paper, Scissors. The winner chooses whether to kick or receive. The normal kickoff and kick-return rules are used, and the receiving team begins the first offensive possession from the resulting field position.
Touchdown: If the team with the opening possession scores a touchdown, the game ends immediately. A touchdown at any later point in overtime also ends the game immediately.
Opening-possession field goal: If the first team scores a field goal, the opponent receives one possession. A touchdown on that possession wins the game. A field goal ties the overtime score and sends the game to sudden death. If the opponent fails to score, the team that made the original field goal wins.
Both teams fail to score: If each team has completed an offensive possession without scoring and the game is still tied, overtime becomes sudden death.
Sudden death: Once sudden death begins, the next score wins — touchdown, field goal, or safety.
Safety: A safety at any point in overtime ends the game immediately. The team scoring the safety wins.
Each team receives 3 timeouts in overtime.
